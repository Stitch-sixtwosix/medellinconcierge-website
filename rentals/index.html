Renting in Medellín as a foreigner

Yes, foreigners can rent an apartment in Medellín. Colombia’s Constitution (Article 100) gives foreigners the same civil rights as Colombians. For newcomers the hard part is usually the paperwork, not the law: for traditional unfurnished leases, many agencies ask for a fiador (a Colombian co-signer), which someone who just arrived rarely has.

A furnished rental booked directly with the team that manages it is the simpler route for most people arriving from abroad: the apartment is ready to live in, you agree the dates and conditions with us in writing, and we answer in English or Spanish. Requirements depend on the apartment and the length of the stay, so ask us what the one you like needs before you book.

  • Short stays: foreign guests are reported to Migración Colombia through SIRE, which we handle as the property manager (as of November 2026), so have your passport at hand.
  • Monthly stays: get a written agreement with the price, what it includes and the dates. Our guide to renting without a fiador explains the usual alternatives, such as a rental insurance policy or rent paid in advance, and what Ley 820 de 2003 says about deposits.
  • Length of stay: how long you can stay in Colombia depends on your migration status; see visitor stays and the digital nomad visa.

This is general information as of November 2026, not legal advice; confirm any contract with a qualified professional.

Before you book a furnished apartment in Medellín: a quick checklist

  • For short stays, check the RNT. Ask for the listing’s RNT number and look it up on the public RNT system.
  • Know the total price. Ask what the price includes (for example utilities, internet and cleaning) and whether anything is charged on top.
  • Agree the dates and rules in writing. Check-in and check-out times, building rules and, for monthly stays, the length of the agreement.
  • Know who to call. Make sure someone answers 24/7 if something goes wrong during your stay.

Rules for tourist rentals can change; this reflects how things stand as of November 2026.

Can foreigners rent an apartment in Medellín?

Yes. Colombia’s Constitution (Article 100) gives foreigners the same civil rights as Colombians. Traditional unfurnished leases often ask for a Colombian co-signer (fiador), so most newcomers start with a furnished apartment booked directly with the manager, by the month or by the night. Do I need a fiador (co-signer) or a deposit for a furnished rental in Medellín?
