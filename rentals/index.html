Short stays and monthly stays

Our apartments are offered for short stays (a few nights) and for monthly stays of 30 nights or more. Short stays are tourist rentals: in Colombia they need a Registro Nacional de Turismo (RNT), and foreign guests are reported to Migración Colombia through SIRE, which we handle as the property manager (as of November 2026). For a monthly stay, make sure you get a clear written agreement; our guide to renting a furnished apartment in Medellín without a fiador explains what to check. Staying several months? Where our furnished apartments are

  • El Poblado: the area with the most restaurants, cafés, coworking spaces and nightlife, and an easy first base in the city.
  • Laureles: flat, tree-lined and residential, with a local feel and plenty of cafés and parks (Medellin Guru).
  • Envigado: a separate municipality just south of Medellín in the Valle de Aburrá (La FM), quieter and good for everyday life.
  • Sabaneta: the smallest municipality in Colombia by area (Blu Radio), with its own station on Metro Line A (Wikipedia).

The listings above show which areas have availability right now. Not sure which neighborhood fits you? Read El Poblado vs Laureles vs Envigado for a month-long stay.

Before you book a furnished apartment in Medellín: a quick checklist

  • For short stays, check the RNT. Ask for the listing’s RNT number and look it up on the public RNT system.
  • Know the total price. Ask what the price includes (for example utilities, internet and cleaning) and whether anything is charged on top.
  • Agree the dates and rules in writing. Check-in and check-out times, building rules and, for monthly stays, the length of the agreement.
  • Know who to call. Make sure someone answers 24/7 if something goes wrong during your stay.

Rules for tourist rentals can change; this reflects how things stand as of November 2026.
