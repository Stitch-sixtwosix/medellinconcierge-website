Managing a Colombian Property From Abroad: RNT, SIRE and Getting Paid

8 November 2026 · 7 min read · Medellín Concierge

Owning an apartment in Medellín while living in Miami, Madrid or Toronto is common, and it can work very well. But distance changes the job. You cannot walk over to meet a guest, check a leak or sign a form at the notary. This guide explains, in plain language, what an owner abroad needs to keep in order: the registrations (RNT, TRA and SIRE), the paperwork around foreign money and taxes, and how to see and receive your income.

Everything below is general information as of November 2026. Rules in Colombia change often, especially for short-term rentals, so confirm the current requirements with us or with the authority before you act. For tax and legal questions, speak with a qualified Colombian accountant or lawyer; we are not tax or legal advisors.

First decision: short-term or long-term?

The obligations depend on how you rent.

  • Short-term (tourist) rentals, such as Airbnb or Booking.com stays, are treated as vivienda turística: a tourism service with its own registrations and guest reporting.
  • Long-term rentals (arriendos of months or years) follow ordinary lease rules and do not need tourism registrations, but they still need a written contract, rent collection, maintenance and someone to deal with the tenant.

Our Medellín property management guide compares both models in detail. The rest of this post focuses on the extra layers that matter when you are not in the country.

The RNT: your tourism registration

If you rent to tourists, the property must be listed in the Registro Nacional de Turismo (RNT). Registration is free and done online through the Confecámaras RNT system, and it must be renewed every year. The Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Tourism (MinCIT) reminds providers that anyone who is not registered, or does not renew, cannot legally provide tourism services.

The requirements for tourist homes are set out in Decreto 1836 de 2021, as MinCIT explains in this 2026 answer to a citizen request. Two points catch many owners by surprise:

  1. Your building has to allow it. The building's rules (reglamento de propiedad horizontal) must permit tourist use.
  2. The RNT is not the same as land-use permission. The Alcaldía de Medellín explains that the RNT is a tourism registration and does not replace land-use (uso del suelo) compliance; both are required. Its November 2026 infographic for tourist homes tells owners to check that the property sits in a zone where the land use is compatible with the activity.

The sector has grown fast. According to the Alcaldía, the number of tourist-home providers registered in the RNT in Medellín went from 521 in 2019 to 8,322 in 2026 (source). With that growth has come more regulation: the city is revising where tourist homes can operate as part of its land-use plan (POT) (Alcaldía de Medellín), and national changes to the RNT have been discussed in the press (El Colombiano). If you are abroad, this is the area most worth having someone watch for you.

TRA and SIRE: reporting every guest

Once you are operating, two guest-reporting duties apply, and both happen at check-in, which is exactly when an owner abroad is least able to act.

  • TRA (Tarjeta de Registro de Alojamiento): MinCIT's guest registration card. The Alcaldía's guide for tourist homes lists implementing the TRA for every guest among the operator's obligations.
  • SIRE (Sistema de Información para el Reporte de Extranjeros): Migración Colombia's platform for reporting foreign nationals. Migración's ABC of SIRE includes accommodation that receives foreign tourists among those who must report. The reporting rules are set in Resolución 2357 de 2020, and the deadlines are short, so in practice someone has to collect each guest's passport details and file the report as soon as they arrive.

The city's guide also mentions other recurring duties, such as the quarterly parafiscal contribution to FONTUR and notifying the nearest police station. Missing any of these is a real risk: the Alcaldía has stepped up enforcement against tourist homes that operate illegally.

Money in and out: foreign investment and taxes

Two topics come up for almost every owner who lives abroad. We share general orientation only; for your own situation, consult a qualified Colombian accountant or lawyer.

Registering your foreign investment. When money from abroad is used to buy property in Colombia, it is normally channelled through the foreign-exchange market and registered with the Banco de la República. ProColombia's Invest in Colombia site has an overview of how to register a foreign investment in Colombia. Keeping that registration in order matters later, for example when you want to send sale proceeds back abroad or apply for certain visas.

Taxes on rental income. The DIAN, Colombia's tax authority, taxes non-residents on their Colombian-source income, and rent from a property in Colombia is Colombian-source income. DIAN guidance also addresses withholding on rent paid to non-residents (DIAN, Oficio 10721 de 2025), and whether you must file a Colombian return depends on your circumstances (DIAN Concepto 801 de 2024, via INCP). Your home country may tax the same income too. This is exactly where a Colombian accountant earns their fee.

Getting paid and staying informed

From abroad, the questions that matter most are simple: how much did the property earn, what did it cost, and when does the money reach me?

A few habits help:

  • Keep one place for every number. Booking income, platform fees, cleaning, utilities, building fees (administración) and repairs should all be recorded where you can see them.
  • Ask for proper monthly statements, not just a bank transfer. With us, owners get an owner portal and monthly GAAP financial statements.
  • Keep documents your accountant will ask for: contracts, invoices, platform payout reports and receipts.
  • Agree in writing how and when payouts happen, and how repair costs are approved.

FAQ

Can I rent my Medellín apartment on Airbnb while living outside Colombia?

Yes, foreign owners can rent to tourists, as long as the property meets the rules: an active RNT renewed every year, a building whose rules allow tourist use, compatible land use, and guest reporting through TRA and SIRE. Someone has to handle check-ins and reports locally, which is where a local manager helps. Requirements can change, so confirm them before you start.

Do I need to be in Colombia to register the RNT or file SIRE reports?

Registration is done online through the Confecámaras RNT system, and SIRE reports are filed on Migración Colombia's online platform. But the reports depend on each guest's arrival, so in practice you need someone in Medellín who can collect guest details and file on time.

Do foreign owners pay tax in Colombia on rental income?

The DIAN taxes non-residents on Colombian-source income, which includes rent from a property in Colombia, and withholding may apply. Whether you must file a return, and how your home country treats the income, depends on your situation. Consult a qualified Colombian accountant; we are not tax advisors.
