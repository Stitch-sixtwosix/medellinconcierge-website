Colombia Digital Nomad Visa: How Long Can You Stay in Medellín? (2026)

14 November 2026 · 7 min read · Medellín Concierge

Short answer: most remote workers from visa-exempt countries can stay in Colombia as visitors for up to 90 days, extend once for up to 90 more, and never exceed 180 days in the same calendar year. If you want to live in Medellín longer, Colombia's Digital Nomad Visa (a type V visa) allows a stay of up to two years while you work remotely for employers or clients outside Colombia. Either way, plan your stay length before you sign for a furnished apartment.

This guide is current as of November 2026. It is general information for travellers and remote workers, not legal, immigration or tax advice. Immigration rules and fees change, so always confirm the current requirements with Cancillería and Migración Colombia, or a qualified immigration lawyer, before you travel.

How long can I stay in Colombia as a tourist?

If your passport is from a country that does not need a visa for short stays, Migración Colombia gives you an entry and stay permit (Permiso de Ingreso y Permanencia, PIP) at the airport, usually for up to 90 days (Migración Colombia, PIP).

You can then ask for an extension of up to 90 more days without leaving the country. As of November 2026, under Migración Colombia Resolution 2061 of 2020, your total stay as a visitor cannot exceed 180 days, continuous or not, in the same calendar year (1 January to 31 December). Leaving the country and coming back does not reset that count.

Practical points:

  • Apply for the extension online with Migración Colombia before your current permit expires, and check its website for how far in advance to apply.
  • Only one extension is granted per entry permit, and applying does not guarantee approval.
  • There is a fee, set each year by the authority. Check the current amount on the Migración Colombia website rather than relying on a blog.

Can I work remotely in Colombia without a visa?

As of November 2026, according to Cancillería's Resolution 5477 of 2022, the resolution that regulates Colombian visas, remote workers from visa-exempt countries can enter with an entry permit as long as they receive no payments from Colombian companies and their stay is no longer than 90 days. If you plan to stay longer than that while working, the Digital Nomad Visa is the route designed for you.

What is the Colombia Digital Nomad Visa?

The Digital Nomad Visa is a visitor visa (type V) created by Resolution 5477 of 2022. Colombia's Ministry of Justice summarizes the process in plain Spanish on its LegalApp guide. Based on the resolution, as of November 2026:

  • Who it is for: people who work remotely, by digital means, exclusively for companies or clients outside Colombia, either as employees or as freelancers, and people starting a digital content or information-technology business.
  • How long: up to two years.
  • Passport: you must hold a passport from a country whose nationals do not need a visa for short stays in Colombia.
  • Proof of work: a letter from the foreign employer or client, in Spanish or English, describing the relationship and how you are paid.
  • Income: the resolution ties the minimum income to Colombia's legal monthly minimum wage (SMMLV); the published text asks for at least three minimum wages a month. The 2026 minimum wage is COP 1,750,905 (Decree 159 of 2026), so three works out to COP 5,252,715 a month. Confirm the current figure with Cancillería before you apply.
  • Health insurance: a policy that covers you in Colombia for the length of the visa.
  • Absences: leaving Colombia for 90 calendar days or more ends the visa early.

You apply online through Cancillería's visa portal. Fees are listed by Cancillería and can differ by nationality, so check the official fee table instead of a third-party guide.

If you get a visa valid for more than three months, Migración Colombia requires you to register and obtain a cédula de extranjería, usually within 15 calendar days. Check the exact steps on the Migración Colombia website.

Do digital nomads pay taxes in Colombia?

A visa and tax residency are different things. As of November 2026, under Article 10 of the Tax Statute, a person who stays in Colombia for more than 183 calendar days, continuous or not, within any 365-day period becomes a Colombian tax resident (DIAN). Tax residents are generally taxed on their worldwide income. If you plan to stay that long, speak to a qualified tax professional in Colombia and in your home country. We are not tax advisors.

What does this mean for renting an apartment in Medellín?

Your immigration status shapes your housing plan:

  1. Match the lease to your permit. If you are on a 90-day entry, book for 90 days or less, or make sure the contract lets you leave early if your extension is not approved.
  2. Keep your passport handy. Lodging providers must report foreign guests to Migración Colombia through the SIRE system, so a legitimate host or manager will ask for your passport details.
  3. Think about the address you will use. Visa and registration forms ask for an address in Colombia; a written rental agreement makes that simple.
  4. Choose the neighborhood for your work routine. If you are still deciding, read our guide El Poblado vs Laureles vs Envigado: where to stay in Medellín for a month, and if a landlord asks you for a co-signer, see how to rent a furnished apartment in Medellín without a fiador.

FAQ

How long can a tourist stay in Colombia in 2026?

Visitors from visa-exempt countries usually get up to 90 days on arrival and can extend once for up to 90 more, with a maximum of 180 days in the same calendar year (Migración Colombia Resolution 2061 of 2020), as of November 2026.

How much income do I need for the Colombia Digital Nomad Visa?

Resolution 5477 of 2022 sets the requirement in multiples of the monthly minimum wage; the published text asks for at least three. With the 2026 minimum wage of COP 1,750,905, that is COP 5,252,715 a month. Confirm the current figure with Cancillería before applying.

Can I rent a furnished apartment in Medellín on a tourist entry?

Yes. Furnished monthly rentals are common for visitors. Keep the rental within your permitted stay, have your passport ready for the SIRE report, and get a written agreement before you pay.
