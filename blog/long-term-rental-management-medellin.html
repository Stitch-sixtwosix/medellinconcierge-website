Long-Term Rental Management in Medellín: What Owners Need to Know

10 November 2026 · 7 min read · Medellín Concierge

Not every Medellín apartment belongs on Airbnb. Some buildings do not allow tourist stays, some owners prefer one steady tenant over a stream of guests, and some simply want fewer moving parts while they live abroad. A long-term lease (an arriendo) can be a good fit, but it comes with its own rulebook in Colombia, and that rulebook favors careful paperwork.

This guide explains, in plain language, the rules that shape long-term rental management in Medellín as of November 2026: how rent increases work, what you can and cannot ask a tenant for, how a lease ends, and what a property manager typically handles. It is general information, not legal advice. Rules change, so confirm the details for your property before you sign anything.

Long-term vs short-term: two different rulebooks

In Colombia, a short-term tourist rental (vivienda turística) is a stay of less than 30 days under Decree 1836 of 2021. It needs an active National Tourism Registry (RNT) from MinCIT, guest reporting, and a building and land use that allow tourist stays.

A long-term residential lease follows a different law: Law 820 of 2003, the national framework for urban housing leases. It sets how rent can rise, what guarantees are allowed, and how either side can end the contract. If you are still deciding between the two models, our Medellín property management guide compares them side by side.

How much can you raise the rent?

This is the question owners ask most, and Law 820 answers it clearly:

  • Once a year. The rent can be increased only after each 12 months of the contract, counted from the start date or from the last legal increase.
  • Capped by inflation. The increase cannot exceed the consumer price index (IPC) of the previous calendar year, as Portafolio's guide to rent increases explains. Owner and tenant can agree on a smaller increase.

For leases renewing in 2026, the reference is 2025 inflation. DANE reported that the IPC closed 2025 at 5.10% (Portafolio, Noticias Caracol). So a residential lease that reaches its anniversary in 2026 can generally rise by up to 5.10%. The 2027 cap will depend on the 2026 inflation figure, which DANE publishes in early January.

Commercial leases (offices, shops) are not under this cap. If you own a mixed-use property, check which rules apply to each contract.

Deposits: why they are different in Colombia

Owners coming from the US, Canada or Europe often expect to collect a cash security deposit. For urban residential leases, Article 16 of Law 820 does not allow it: landlords cannot require cash deposits or other real guarantees (cauciones reales) to secure the tenant's obligations, as the Bogotá Hábitat landlord guide summarizes.

Instead, Colombian leases usually rely on personal guarantees: a co-signer (codeudor or fiador) or a rental guarantee policy (póliza de arrendamiento) from an insurer. Which option is available, and how strong it is, depends on the tenant and the insurer. This is one of the main reasons screening matters so much in long-term rentals here.

How a long-term lease ends

Ending a lease is where informal arrangements cause the most trouble. A few points from Law 820, as explained in this CMS Law analysis and the Constitutional Court's ruling C-426 of 2023:

  • Written notice matters. If the required written notice is not given, the contract generally renews automatically for the same term.
  • Ending during an extension has a cost. During the contract's extensions, the landlord can end it unilaterally with at least three months' written notice and an indemnity equal to three months' rent, deposited in favor of the tenant under the procedure in Article 23.
  • Other grounds exist, such as needing the property for the owner's own home, major works or a sale, each with its own notice and conditions.

These steps are formal. Missing a date or sending notice the wrong way can extend a lease you meant to end. For any specific termination, talk to a Colombian lawyer first.

Do you need to register as a landlord?

Article 28 of Law 820 created a landlord registry (matrícula de arrendadores). Under the regulation now compiled in Decree 1077 of 2015, it applies to people or companies that, as owners or sublessors, hold more than five lease contracts, on their own properties or third parties' (Decree 51 of 2004, Función Pública). The registration itself has no cost.

In Medellín, the Alcaldía handles it as an official procedure: see the city's Matrícula de arrendadores page. An owner renting one or two apartments directly is usually below the threshold, but confirm your own case with the Alcaldía.

What a long-term property manager typically handles

Day to day, long-term rental management in Medellín usually covers:

  1. Preparing the unit. Repairs, cleaning, and photos and a listing that show the apartment well.
  2. Finding and screening tenants. Checking income, references and the guarantee (co-signer or rental policy) before a lease is signed.
  3. The lease and the inventory. A written contract and a detailed move-in inventory with photos, so the move-out comparison is clear.
  4. Rent and building fees. Tracking monthly rent and the building's administration fee (cuota de administración), and following up on late payments.
  5. Maintenance. Coordinating repairs with trusted vendors and the building administration.
  6. Annual increases and renewals. Applying the IPC increase on the right date and sending notices on time.
  7. Reporting. Clear monthly numbers so you know what the property earned and spent.

If you live outside Colombia, the last three items are the hardest to do from a distance, and the ones where missed dates cost the most.

FAQ

How much can I raise the rent on a long-term lease in Medellín in 2026?

For residential leases, the increase is limited to the previous year's IPC and can be applied once every 12 months. DANE reported 2025 inflation at 5.10%, so leases reaching their anniversary in 2026 can generally rise by up to that amount. Owner and tenant can agree on less.

Can I ask a tenant in Colombia for a security deposit?

Not a cash deposit for a residential lease: Article 16 of Law 820 of 2003 prohibits cash deposits and real guarantees. Owners usually use a co-signer (codeudor) or a rental guarantee policy instead.

Is long-term or short-term rental better for my Medellín apartment?

It depends on your building's rules, the land use, the neighborhood and how hands-on you want to be. Short-term rentals need an RNT and guest reporting; long-term leases follow Law 820. Our Medellín property management guide compares both, and we are happy to look at your specific property.
