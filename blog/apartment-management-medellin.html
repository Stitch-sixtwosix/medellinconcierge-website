Apartment Management in Medellín: What a Manager Does and What It Costs (2026)

23 November 2026 · 7 min read · Medellín Concierge

Short answer: in Medellín, "administración" can mean two very different things. One is the building fee (cuota de administración), which you pay to your building or complex (the propiedad horizontal, roughly an HOA) for common areas, security and upkeep. The other is rental management of your own apartment: a company that finds and looks after tenants or guests, collects rent, maintains the unit and reports to you, usually for a percentage of rental income plus VAT. They are different contracts, costs and people.

This guide explains what each one does, what it costs and what to check before you sign. For the big picture, start with our Medellín property management guide.

General information as of November 2026. This is not legal or tax advice: we are not tax advisors or lawyers, so please consult a qualified professional about your situation.

What is the building fee, and who charges it?

If your apartment is in a building or gated complex under Colombia's horizontal property regime, Law 675 of 2001 (in Spanish) sets the rules. Under that law:

  • The building's administrator is appointed by the owners' assembly or, where one exists, by the board (consejo de administración) (Article 50).
  • That administrator has powers of execution, upkeep, representation and collection for the building (Article 51). In other words, they work for the building as a whole, not for you as an individual owner.
  • Every owner must contribute to common expenses, even if they don't live in the unit, and for ordinary expenses the owner and the occupant are jointly liable (Article 29).

That last point matters if you rent out: even if the lease says the tenant pays the fee, the building can also collect it from you. Someone should check every month that it is paid.

No outside company sets the fee. Each building's assembly approves it with the yearly budget, so it varies a lot from one building to the next.

What does a rental apartment manager do?

This is the company or person you hire so your apartment earns income without you being there. Depending on the rental model, the work usually looks like this:

Finding occupantsAdvertising, showings and tenant screeningListings on Airbnb, Booking.com, Expedia and VRBO
PricingMonthly rent and the yearly legal increaseDynamic nightly and seasonal pricing

MaintenanceRepairs owed by the landlordRepairs and restocking supplies

For short stays, the apartment also needs a National Tourism Registry (RNT) number, which according to MinCIT (as of November 2026) is renewed every year between January 1 and March 31, and your building's rules must allow tourist use. We cover that in Airbnb requirements in Medellín.

How much does an apartment manager in Medellín charge?

There is no official rate. The usual model is a percentage: of the monthly rent for long-term rentals, or of booking income for short-term rentals. VAT (IVA) is added to that commission when the company is registered for it; Colombia's general rate is 19% under Article 468 of the Tax Statute.

Before comparing quotes, ask what amount the percentage is calculated on (gross, after the platform's commission, or after costs), what it includes and what is billed separately. In how much Airbnb management costs in Medellín we show with an example how that base changes your real cost.

Which costs stay with the owner?

Even with a manager, the apartment's own costs are still yours (on the Premium plan we pay them for you out of rental income, but they don't go away):

  • The building fee (cuota de administración).
  • Property tax (see property tax in Medellín).
  • Utilities and internet, if the tenant doesn't pay them.
  • Repairs and maintenance.
  • For short-term rentals, each platform's commission and guest supplies.
  • Taxes on your rental income, which depend on your situation: ask an accountant.

How can you tell whether a rental agency is registered?

Law 820 of 2003 (Article 28, in Spanish) requires anyone in the business of renting out other people's property to hold a landlord registration (matrícula de arrendador). Decree 51 of 2004 says they must register in every municipality where they operate and show the registration number in their advertising. In Medellín the registration is handled by the Alcaldía; see the official matrícula de arrendadores page. If a company won't show you its number, ask why.

Checklist before you sign with a manager

  • What is the percentage, what is it calculated on, and does it include IVA?
  • Which services are included, and which are extra (cleaning, repairs, supplies)?
  • Does the company hold a landlord registration, if it manages long-term leases?
  • For short-term rentals: who keeps the RNT, SIRE and TRA up to date?
  • Who checks that the building fee and property tax are paid?
  • How often are you paid, and to which account (including one abroad)?
  • What report do you get each month, and can you see bookings or payments online?
  • How long is the contract, and how do you end it?

FAQ

Does the owner or the tenant pay the building fee in Medellín?
A lease can pass it to the tenant, but under Article 29 of Law 675 of 2001 the owner and the occupant are jointly liable for ordinary common expenses, so the building can collect it from you. Check every month that it is paid.

How much does an apartment manager charge in Medellín?
There is no official rate; it is usually a percentage of the rent or of bookings, plus IVA where it applies. Our plans, as of November 2026, run from 12% + IVA (long-term Basic) to 20% + IVA (short-term Premium); see our plans and the fees page.

Can my building's administrator rent out my apartment?
That is a different role. The building administrator works for the building as a whole (Article 51 of Law 675). To rent out your apartment you need your own manager or agency, with a contract signed with you.
