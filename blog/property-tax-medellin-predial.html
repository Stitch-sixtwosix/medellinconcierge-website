Property Tax in Medellín (Impuesto Predial): How It's Calculated, When It's Due and How to Pay From Abroad (2026)

19 November 2026 · 6 min read · Medellín Concierge

Short answer: property tax in Medellín (the impuesto predial) is a yearly municipal tax that the owner pays, calculated on the property's cadastral value. In 2026 the city billed it in four quarterly installments and gave a 5% discount to owners who paid the whole year by March 27 and were up to date on earlier years. You can pay online, in the HaciendaMed app or at banks, including from abroad if you have an accepted payment method.

This guide is for owners, in Colombia or overseas, who want to understand their predial bill and avoid surprises. It is general information as of November 2026, not tax advice.

What is the predial, and who has to pay it?

The impuesto predial unificado is a tax each municipality charges on the real estate inside its borders. If your apartment is in El Poblado or Laureles, the District of Medellín bills you. If it is in Envigado or Sabaneta, that municipality bills you, with its own calendar and its own discounts.

The owner pays it, Colombian or foreign, living in Medellín or not. It doesn't matter whether the unit is empty, on a long-term lease or on Airbnb: the obligation stays with the owner. Don't confuse it with income tax on your rental income, which is filed with DIAN and depends on your tax residency.

How is property tax calculated in Medellín?

The basic formula is cadastral value (avalúo catastral) × rate (per thousand).

  • Cadastral value: the value Medellín's cadastre assigns to your property. It is usually different from the market price. The city explains how cadastral values are adjusted each year and when a full cadastral update happens.
  • Rate: set by Medellín's city council in the District's tax code. It depends on how the property is used (residential, commercial, vacant land) and, for homes, on the estrato and the value bracket. Your bill shows the rate applied.

How much can the predial go up from one year to the next?

When a city updates its cadastre, values can jump. To soften that, as of November 2026, Law 1995 of 2019 caps how much the tax itself can rise year on year: generally by inflation (IPC) only for estrato 1 and 2 homes valued up to 135 minimum wages, and by inflation plus eight points for other properties, with exceptions (for example a change of use or new construction). The cap applies to the tax, not to the cadastral value.

In 2026 Medellín kept rolling out its cadastral update comuna by comuna, applying those limits (Alcaldía de Medellín). If your comuna was updated, read your 2027 bill carefully when it arrives.

The city sets the predial calendar and any discount each year, so don't assume 2027 will match 2026. If it follows the 2026 pattern, the key decision comes in the first quarter: pay the full year with the discount, or pay by installment. Confirm the dates in the official calendar once it is out.

How do you pay Medellín property tax from abroad?

The city lists these payment channels:

  1. Online at medellin.gov.co/pagoimpuestos, through PSE or Place to Pay, with a debit or credit card.
  2. The HaciendaMed app, to check and pay from your phone.
  3. In person at authorized banks and bank correspondents.

Checklist for owners who live overseas

  • Keep your matrícula inmobiliaria (registration number) or código predial (CBML) handy. Both appear on your deed and your certificado de tradición y libertad.
  • Check that the email and mailing address on file with the city are yours, so the bill reaches you.
  • Decide how you will pay: PSE works with Colombian bank accounts. If you only have a foreign card, test Place to Pay well before the deadline, or put someone you trust in charge of the payment.
  • Put the first-quarter annual discount in your calendar.
  • Save every payment receipt, and if you plan to sell, request the paz y salvo (tax clearance) early.
  • Read each year's bill: if the cadastral value or the amount changes a lot, ask Medellín's cadastre office.

What happens if you don't pay?

Unpaid predial accrues late interest, and the city can start a collection process (cobro coactivo). If you plan to sell, you will usually need a predial paz y salvo, so confirm the requirements with your notary. Arrears also cost you the early-payment discount, which in 2026 was only for owners up to date. Property tax is one of the running costs to include when you work out your real rental yield in Medellín.

FAQ

Do foreigners pay property tax in Medellín?
Yes. The predial is paid by the property owner, whatever their nationality or where they live. The rate and calculation are the same as for a Colombian owner.

When will Medellín announce the 2027 predial discount?
The city sets the calendar and any discount each year. In 2026 the discount was 5% for paying the full year by March 27, for owners up to date on earlier years. Check the city's tax calendar in early 2027.

Is property tax for Envigado or Sabaneta paid to Medellín?
No. Each municipality collects its own predial, with its own calendar and payment channels. If your property is in Envigado or Sabaneta, contact that municipality's treasury office (Secretaría de Hacienda).
