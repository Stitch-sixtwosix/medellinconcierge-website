Airbnb Management in Medellín: What a Manager Does and What the Fees Cover

6 November 2026 · 7 min read · Medellín Concierge

If you own an apartment in El Poblado, Laureles, Envigado or Sabaneta and want to rent it on Airbnb, sooner or later you will ask the same question most owners ask: should I run it myself, or hand it to a manager? And if I hand it over, what exactly am I paying for?

This article explains what Airbnb management in Medellín usually includes, how managers and co-hosts are typically paid, how Airbnb's own fees fit in, and the questions worth asking before you sign anything. It is part of our Medellín property management guide, which covers short-term and long-term rentals in more depth.

This is general information as of November 2026, not legal or tax advice. Platform rules and Colombian regulations change, so confirm the current situation for your property with us before you act.

Manager or co-host: what is the difference?

On Airbnb, a co-host is a person or company the listing owner invites to help run a listing. Airbnb lets the owner choose what a co-host can access and, if they agree, pay the co-host directly through the platform (Airbnb Help Center: how co-host payouts work).

A property manager usually does the same work and more, often across several platforms (Airbnb, Booking.com, Expedia, VRBO), and with responsibilities that go beyond the listing itself: cleaning teams, maintenance, building rules, registrations and owner reporting. In practice, many managers in Medellín also act as co-hosts on the owner's Airbnb account, so the two words overlap.

What matters is not the label but the scope: what will this person or company actually do, and what will stay on your plate?

What an Airbnb manager in Medellín typically does

A full-service manager normally takes care of the whole guest cycle:

  • Launching the listing. Styling, professional photos and a listing that describes the property honestly, so guests get what they expect.
  • Pricing. Adjusting nightly rates for seasons, holidays, events and demand. Medellín has busy periods around events such as the Feria de las Flores in August and the December holidays, and prices should move with them.
  • Guest communication, 24/7. Answering questions before booking, sending check-in instructions, and handling problems at any hour, in English and Spanish.
  • Check-in coordination. Working with the building's porter (portería) and administration so guests are registered and let in smoothly.
  • Cleaning and laundry. Turnovers between stays, linens and restocking basics.
  • Maintenance. Inspections, small repairs, and coordinating technicians when something breaks.
  • Compliance. Keeping the property's National Tourism Registry (RNT) up to date and reporting foreign guests through Migración Colombia's SIRE system. According to MinCIT, the RNT is free, done online and renewed every year between 1 January and 31 March. Migración Colombia operates SIRE for reporting foreign guests.
  • Reviews and reputation. Responding to reviews and fixing whatever guests repeatedly mention.
  • Money and reporting. Tracking income and expenses and sending the owner a clear statement every month.

How Airbnb management fees are usually structured

There is no official or standard fee for managing an Airbnb in Medellín. Fees are agreed privately between the owner and the manager, and they vary with the scope of service, the property and the company. That is why it pays to understand the common structures before comparing offers.

Percentage of booking income

The most common model is a percentage of what each booking earns. Airbnb's own co-host tools reflect this: the owner can pay a co-host a percentage of each booking, a fixed amount, the cleaning fee, or the cleaning fee plus a percentage, and the payout is calculated from the host's earnings for each reservation (Airbnb Help Center).

When you compare percentages, always ask: a percentage of what? Gross booking value, earnings after Airbnb's fee, or earnings after cleaning? The same percentage can mean very different amounts.

Fixed monthly fee

Some managers charge a flat monthly amount instead. It is predictable, but it does not rise or fall with how well the property performs.

Setup and one-off costs

Launching a new listing can involve one-off costs: furniture and equipment, photography, and initial registrations. Ask which of these are included and which are billed separately.

What is usually billed on top

Even with management in place, the owner normally pays the property's own running costs: building administration fees, utilities, internet, supplies, repairs and taxes. Cleaning is often charged per stay, and frequently passed on to guests as a cleaning fee.

Do not forget Airbnb's own fee

The manager's fee is separate from what Airbnb charges. Airbnb has two structures: a split fee, where the host pays a small part and the guest pays a service fee, and a single fee paid entirely by the host (Airbnb Help Center: what are Airbnb service fees). In 2025 Airbnb moved most hosts, starting with those using property-management software, to the host-only fee; El Colombiano reported that hosts in Colombia now absorb a fee of about 15.5% that guests used to pay on top of the price.

In practice, this means the nightly rate you set has to cover Airbnb's fee, the manager's fee and your costs. A good manager will show you how they price with that in mind.

Questions to ask before you hire an Airbnb manager in Medellín

  1. What exactly is included? Get the list of services in writing, including who handles RNT renewal and SIRE reports.
  2. What is the fee based on? Gross bookings, net payout, or something else? Are cleaning fees included in the base?
  3. Who owns the listing and the reviews? Ideally the listing stays on your account and the manager is a co-host, so you keep your reviews if you ever change manager.
  4. How and when do I get paid? Ask how money flows, in which currency, and how often you receive statements.
  5. How are repairs approved? Is there a spending limit above which they need your approval?
  6. Can my building legally host short stays? Many buildings have rules in their reglamento de propiedad horizontal. Check before you invest in furnishing.
  7. What happens if I want to leave? Ask about notice periods and how future bookings are handled.

For tax questions, such as income tax on rental income or VAT on tourist accommodation, speak to a qualified Colombian tax professional. We are not tax advisors.

Is professional Airbnb management worth it?

It depends on your situation. If you live nearby, speak Spanish and enjoy hosting, you may prefer to run the listing yourself. If you live abroad, own several units or simply do not want calls at 2 a.m., a manager can take on the daily work while you keep visibility through reports.

FAQ

How much does an Airbnb manager charge in Medellín?

There is no standard rate. Managers usually charge a percentage of booking income or a fixed monthly fee, and what is included varies widely. Compare offers by asking what the percentage is calculated on and what is billed separately. Our own plans are listed at /#plans.

Is a co-host the same as a property manager?

Not exactly. A co-host is someone the owner adds to an Airbnb listing to help run it. A property manager usually also handles cleaning teams, maintenance, multiple platforms, compliance and owner reporting, and often works as a co-host on the owner's account.

Who pays Airbnb's service fee?

As of November 2026, most hosts in Colombia are on Airbnb's single, host-only fee, which comes out of the host's payout (El Colombiano). Check your own listing settings, because Airbnb's fee structure can vary by listing and change over time.
