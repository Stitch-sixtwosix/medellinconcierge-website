Medellín Property Management: The Complete Guide for Owners

5 November 2026 · 7 min read · Medellín Concierge

Owning an apartment in Medellín, Colombia, is the easy part. Keeping it rented, legal, well maintained and profitable, especially if you live in another country, is where most of the work is. This guide explains what Medellín property management actually covers, the two main ways to rent out a property (short-term and long-term), the rules that apply to each as of November 2026, and how to choose a manager you can trust.

It is written for owners and investors in El Poblado, Laureles, Envigado and Sabaneta, whether you already own or are still shopping. It is general information, not legal or tax advice, and regulations change, so confirm the current rules for your property with us before you act.

What does a property manager in Medellín do?

A good manager takes over the day-to-day running of the property so the owner does not have to be in the city. Depending on the type of rental, that usually includes:

  • Getting the property ready. Furnishing and equipping an empty unit, professional photos, and a listing or advertisement that matches the property.
  • Finding guests or tenants. Publishing on Airbnb, Booking.com, Expedia and VRBO for short stays, or screening tenants for a long-term lease.
  • Pricing. Adjusting nightly rates for season, events and demand, or setting a monthly rent that fits the market and the law.
  • Communication. Answering guests around the clock, or being the tenant's single point of contact.
  • Cleaning and maintenance. Turnovers between stays, inspections, repairs and coordination with the building administration.
  • Compliance. Registrations and reports required by Colombian authorities (more on this below).
  • Money and reporting. Collecting income, paying building fees and utilities, and sending the owner a clear monthly statement.

Short-term or long-term: the first decision

Most owners in Medellín choose between two models. Neither is "better"; the right one depends on your building, your goals and how hands-off you want to be.

Short-term rentals (vivienda turística)

Renting by the night or week to travellers through platforms like Airbnb. It usually involves more work (frequent cleaning, guest messages, pricing every day) and more regulation, but gives you flexibility to use the property yourself and adjust prices to demand.

In Colombia, a home rented to tourists is a vivienda turística, and it must be registered in the National Tourism Registry (RNT) before it operates. According to the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Tourism (MinCIT), the RNT is free, is done online and must be renewed every year between 1 January and 31 March. On top of that, every stay must be reported through the TRA (MinCIT's accommodation registration card), foreign guests must be reported to Migración Colombia through SIRE, and, if the apartment is in a building under propiedad horizontal, the building's bylaws must allow tourist use.

The Alcaldía de Medellín has also stepped up inspections of informal tourist rentals, so compliance is not optional.

Long-term rentals (arriendo de vivienda)

Renting to one tenant for months or years under a lease. There is less day-to-day work and no guest turnover, but less flexibility, and the lease is governed by Law 820 of 2003, the national law on urban housing leases. Two points every owner should know:

  • Rent cap. Article 18 says the monthly rent cannot exceed 1% of the property's commercial value (and that estimated value cannot exceed twice the official cadastral value).
  • Annual increases. Article 20 allows the owner to raise the rent once every 12 months, by no more than the previous year's consumer price inflation (IPC), as long as the new rent stays within the Article 18 cap.

The law also covers deposits, contract renewals, termination notices and eviction procedures, which is why most foreign owners prefer a local manager to handle the lease.

Furnished mid-term stays

Between the two sits a growing middle ground: furnished stays of one month or more for remote workers and professionals. These can mean fewer turnovers than nightly rentals, but the right legal and contractual setup depends on how the stay is structured. Ask us before you switch models.

The rules for managers themselves

In Colombia, people and companies that rent out homes on behalf of others, or act as intermediaries between owners and tenants, must be registered with the local authority. In Medellín this is the matrícula de arrendadores, a procedure handled by the Alcaldía. It exists under Law 820 of 2003 so the city can supervise rental intermediaries. When you compare managers for long-term rentals, asking about this registration is a simple and reasonable check.

Managing your property from abroad

Many owners of property in Medellín live abroad. Owning from abroad is entirely possible, but plan for a few extra things:

  • Someone on the ground. Leaks, lost keys and building meetings do not wait for your next visit.
  • Banking and payments. Building fees (administración), utilities and property tax (predial) must be paid on time in Colombian pesos.
  • Foreign investment registration. Foreign owners usually need their investment properly registered with the Banco de la República (generally done through the foreign-exchange declaration when the funds come in through an authorized bank), so they can later take money out of the country. Ask your lawyer or advisor how this applies to your purchase.
  • Taxes. Rental income from Colombian property is generally taxable in Colombia. For non-residents, DIAN guidance (for example Oficio 2179 of 2019) explains that withholding tax and filing duties depend on the specific situation. You may also have obligations in your home country. Get advice from a qualified tax professional; we are not tax advisors.

How to choose a property manager in Medellín

Whether you are comparing inmobiliarias, Airbnb co-hosts or full-service companies, these questions help:

  1. What exactly is included? Listing, pricing, cleaning, maintenance, guest messages, compliance, reporting. Get it in writing.
  2. How are fees structured? Percentage of income, fixed fee, or a mix, and which costs (cleaning, supplies, repairs) are charged separately.
  3. Who handles RNT, TRA and SIRE? For short-term rentals this should be a clear yes.
  4. Are they registered? For long-term leases, ask about the matrícula de arrendadores.
  5. How will you see your numbers? Monthly statements, an owner portal, access to booking data.
  6. What areas do they really know? Rules, demand and buildings differ between El Poblado, Laureles, Envigado and Sabaneta.
  7. What does the contract say about leaving? Notice periods and how your listing and data are handed back.

FAQ

How much does property management cost in Medellín?

It varies by model and scope. Short-term management is usually priced as a percentage of rental income, and long-term management as a percentage of the monthly rent, with cleaning and repairs often billed separately. Ask any manager for a written breakdown. Our own fees are published on our plans page, and we are happy to walk you through them in a free consultation.

Can a foreigner own and rent out property in Medellín?

Yes. Foreigners can buy property in Colombia and rent it out. The same rules apply as for Colombians (RNT and guest reporting for short stays, Law 820 of 2003 for long leases), plus foreign investment registration and tax duties that depend on your residency.

Is short-term or long-term rental better in Medellín?

It depends on whether your building allows tourist use, how much involvement you want and how you plan to use the property. A manager can compare both options for your specific unit before you commit.
