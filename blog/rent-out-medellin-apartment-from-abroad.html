How to Rent Out Your Medellín Apartment While Living Abroad: Power of Attorney, Contract and Payments (2026)

21 November 2026 · 8 min read · Medellín Concierge

Short answer: yes, you can rent out an apartment in Medellín without being in Colombia. Usually someone in the city acts for you under a power of attorney (poder) signed abroad, either at a Colombian consulate or before a local notary with an apostille; the lease is signed remotely or by your attorney-in-fact or manager; and the rent reaches you by bank transfer with a monthly income and expense report.

This guide walks through those three steps: who signs for you, what a manager can do while you are away, and how you get paid. It is general information as of November 2026, not legal or tax advice. Consular procedures and rules change, so confirm current requirements with your consulate and a qualified lawyer in Colombia before you sign anything.

For the full picture of registrations (RNT, TRA, SIRE) and taxes for overseas owners, start with our guide to managing a Colombian property from abroad. This article focuses on the practical side: signatures, contracts and payments.

Do I need a power of attorney to rent out my apartment from abroad?

Not always, but it almost always helps. There are two ways to work:

  1. You sign everything remotely. You e-sign the lease and your management agreement, and the manager runs day-to-day operations under that agreement.
  2. You give a power of attorney to someone you trust or to your manager. The attorney-in-fact can sign the acts the poder lists on your behalf: the lease, the handover, paperwork with the building administration or utility companies.

A poder matters most when a party asks for an in-person or authenticated signature, or when something needs signing quickly and you are in another time zone.

How do I sign a power of attorney outside Colombia?

Colombia's Foreign Ministry (Cancillería) has explained that a poder to be used in Colombia can be granted abroad before a Colombian consul or before the official that local law authorizes (Foreign Ministry Concept 16 of 2010; the national legal defense agency repeats the rule in its Circular 06 of 2023). What the consulates themselves publish:

  • Bring the poder unsigned plus valid ID; you sign in front of the officer. See, for example, the signature-recognition guides of the Colombian Consulate in Frankfurt and the Consulate in Brussels. Both 2024 guides list a fee of EUR 18 per signature; fees vary and each consulate publishes its own, so check yours.
  • Special vs general poder. The Consulate in Toronto.pdf) handles a special poder through signature recognition and a general poder through a public deed, and warns that a general poder carries high risks. It also notes that the consulate does not review what your poder says; that is up to you and your lawyer.
  • The apostille comes from the country where you signed. The Cancillería only apostilles Colombian public documents that will be used abroad (Concept 16 of 2010). If you sign before a notary in the US or Spain, that country's competent authority issues the apostille.

Practical tip: for renting, a special poder with specific powers is usually more prudent than a general one. Have a lawyer in Colombia review the wording, and ask the notary or office where it will be used whether they accept it as drafted.

What a poder to rent your property should cover

A lawyer drafts it for your case, but it should make clear:

  • Your details and your attorney-in-fact's, plus the apartment's address and property registration number (matrícula inmobiliaria).
  • What the attorney-in-fact may do: sign the lease, receive and hand over the property, sign inventories, handle utilities.
  • What they may not do: sell, mortgage or collect money other than rent, if that is your wish.
  • How long the poder is valid.

Can I sign the lease remotely?

As of November 2026, Colombia's Law 527 of 1999 recognizes data messages and electronic signatures, and Decree 2364 of 2012 (now compiled in Decree 1074 of 2015) gives an electronic signature the same effect as a handwritten one if the method reliably identifies the signer. So use an e-signature platform that records identity, date and acceptance, not just a PDF emailed back and forth.

Long-term residential leases are governed by Law 820 of 2003, with its own rules on rent increases, guarantees and termination. Our guide to long-term rental management in Medellín covers them, including why cash deposits are not allowed on residential leases.

What can a manager do while I am not in Medellín?

With a clear management agreement (and a poder where needed), a manager in the city can handle almost everything:

  • Short-term rentals: list the property, adjust pricing, support guests 24/7, register every guest in SIRE and TRA, coordinate cleaning and maintenance. See our Airbnb management in Medellín.
  • Long-term rentals: show the property, screen the tenant, prepare the lease, hand over with an inventory and photos, collect rent and handle repairs. See our long-term property management in Medellín.
  • Both: pay the property bills you authorize and send you reports.

Keep for yourself, or for a specific poder: selling, mortgaging, changing the property's use or suing a tenant. For those, always use a lawyer.

How do I get paid?

Agree on this in writing from day one. A common setup:

  1. Rent or booking payouts go into an account identified for your property.
  2. Expenses you approved are deducted (management fee, cleaning, utilities, building fees, repairs).
  3. You receive the balance on a fixed date, to an account in Colombia or abroad, as agreed and as your bank allows.
  4. Each month you get a statement with receipts.

Two points for your accountant: registering your foreign investment with Banco de la República if you bought with money from abroad, and withholding tax and income tax filing for a non-resident in Colombia. We summarize both, with DIAN sources, in our guide for owners abroad. We are not tax advisors: consult a qualified Colombian accountant or tax lawyer. And don't forget property tax (impuesto predial), which stays your responsibility while you live abroad.

Checklist before renting from abroad

  • Decide: short-term or long-term rental
  • Check the building's bylaws (and RNT, if short-term)
  • Special poder signed at the consulate, or before a notary with an apostille, reviewed by a lawyer
  • Management agreement signed: services, fee, expenses, payout date
  • Lease ready for a reliable e-signature or your attorney-in-fact's signature
  • Account to receive funds and transfer method agreed
  • Photo inventory at handover
  • Colombian accountant for taxes and foreign exchange

FAQ

Can I rent out my Medellín apartment if I live abroad?

Yes. You can sign remotely or give a power of attorney to someone you trust or to your manager to sign and hand over the property for you. For short-term rentals, the property also needs an RNT and building rules that allow it.

Where do I sign a power of attorney if I live outside Colombia?

At a Colombian consulate (by appointment) or before a notary where you live. In the second case, the document usually needs that country's apostille. Confirm requirements with your consulate and a lawyer in Colombia, as of November 2026.

Is an electronically signed lease valid in Colombia?

Law 527 of 1999 and Decree 2364 of 2012 recognize electronic signatures when the method reliably identifies the signer. Use a platform that records identity and acceptance, and consult a lawyer if in doubt.
