Short-Term Rentals in Medellín: How to Rent an Apartment for a Week or Two (2026)

26 November 2026 · 7 min read · Medellín Concierge

Short answer: In Medellín, a stay of a week or two in a furnished apartment counts as tourist accommodation (vivienda turística), because Colombian rules define it as a stay of less than 30 days (Decree 1836 of 2021). That means the apartment should have a National Tourism Registry (RNT) number, the host should register you on arrival, and the building and zone must allow short stays. Check those three things before you pay, and you'll avoid most of the problems travellers run into.

This guide is for travellers, remote workers, families and anyone visiting Medellín for one to three weeks. It is general information as of November 2026, not legal advice. Rules change, so confirm the details with your host before booking, and consult a qualified professional if you have a legal question.

Is it legal to rent an apartment for a week in Medellín?

Yes, when the apartment is operated legally. As of November 2026, a legal short-term rental in Medellín needs, among other things:

  • An RNT number. Tourist accommodation providers must register in the National Tourism Registry before offering stays (MinCIT). Under Law 2068 of 2020 and Decree 1836 of 2021, booking platforms must show the provider's RNT number and take down listings that don't comply (MinCIT).
  • Guest registration. The Alcaldía de Medellín lists the Tarjeta de Registro de Alojamiento (TRA) for every guest and, for foreign guests, the SIRE report to Migración Colombia among the requirements for short-term rentals (Alcaldía de Medellín).
  • The right building and zone. Under the city's current land-use plan (POT, Acuerdo 48 of 2014), tourist housing is not allowed in zones that are mainly residential, and a building's own rules (reglamento de propiedad horizontal) can restrict or ban stays under 30 days (Alcaldía de Medellín).

The city does enforce this. As reported by Minuto30, the Alcaldía counted 408 inspections of tourist apartments between 2024 and 2026, with 105 temporarily suspended for breaking the rules (Minuto30). For a guest, the practical risk of an informal listing is simple: a building that turns you away at the door, or a host who cancels at short notice.

How do I check that a short-term rental is legitimate?

Use this checklist before you send any money:

  1. Find the RNT number on the listing or ask the host for it.
  2. Look it up on the public Confecámaras RNT system. A MinCIT answer to a citizen request shows that the lookup returns the category, status and validity dates (MinCIT). Check that it says active.
  3. Ask whether the building allows stays under 30 days. A clear yes from the host is worth more than a nice photo.
  4. Expect to show ID at check-in. The Alcaldía's guide asks hosts to check every guest's identity document on arrival, including for children (Alcaldía de Medellín). Foreign visitors should bring their passport, because the host must report your arrival and departure through SIRE (Migración Colombia).
  5. Get the full price in writing: nights, cleaning, deposit, taxes and any platform fee.
  6. Pay by a traceable method, through the platform or to a registered business, never by cash handed over at the door.

Where should I stay for a week or two?

Short stays are about location and convenience more than space. A quick guide to the four areas visitors ask about most:

  • El Poblado: the most tourist-oriented area, with restaurants, nightlife and many apartment buildings. Popular for first visits. See El Poblado apartments for rent.
  • Laureles: flatter, greener and more residential, with plenty of cafés. A common choice for remote workers.
  • Envigado and Sabaneta: separate municipalities south of Medellín, quieter and family-friendly, connected to the city by the Metro. Check which municipality's rules apply to the apartment, since Medellín's POT covers Medellín only.

FAQ

Can I rent an apartment in Medellín for just one week?

Yes. Stays under 30 days are tourist accommodation, so look for an apartment with an active RNT number in a building that allows short stays. Check the number on the Confecámaras RNT system before paying.

Do I need my passport to check in?

Bring it. Hosts must register every guest, and foreign guests must be reported to Migración Colombia through SIRE, so you'll be asked for your ID on arrival.

Is a monthly rental cheaper than two or three weeks on a nightly rate?

It depends on the apartment and the season. For stays close to a month, ask for both prices: a monthly rental (30 days or more) has one price and may open up buildings that don't accept short stays. Email us your dates and we'll compare both for you.
