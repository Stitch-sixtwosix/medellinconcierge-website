Is Airbnb Legal in Medellín? RNT, TRA and SIRE Explained for Property Owners

4 November 2026 · 7 min read · Medellín Concierge

If you own an apartment in El Poblado, Laureles, Envigado or Sabaneta, or you are thinking of buying one, the first question is usually the same: is Airbnb legal in Medellín?

The short answer, as of November 2026: yes, short-term rentals are legal in Medellín and the rest of Colombia, but only when the property is properly registered and allowed by its building rules. Renting by the night without that paperwork is what the authorities treat as informal or illegal operation.

This guide explains the main requirements in plain language: the RNT (National Tourism Registry), the TRA guest card, SIRE reporting for foreign guests, and the building rules that catch many owners by surprise. It is general information, not legal or tax advice. Rules change, so confirm the current situation with us or with the official sources linked below before you list.

The legal framework in one paragraph

In Colombia, a home rented to tourists for short stays is a vivienda turística (tourist home). It is regulated under the General Tourism Law as amended by Law 2068 of 2020 and by the rules for tourist-home providers in Decree 1836 of 2021, which is part of the tourism sector's single regulatory decree (Decree 1074 of 2015). The national authority is the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Tourism (MinCIT). Locally, the Alcaldía de Medellín carries out inspections and has publicly stepped up controls on informal tourist homes.

Requirement 1: the RNT (Registro Nacional de Turismo)

The RNT is the basic license to operate. Under Decree 1836 of 2021, getting the registration is a prior and mandatory requirement before a property can be used as a tourist home.

Key points, according to MinCIT:

  • It is free. There is no registration fee for the RNT itself.
  • It is done online through the chambers of commerce platform at rnt.confecamaras.co.
  • It must be renewed every year, during the first three months (1 January to 31 March). MinCIT reminds providers each year to renew on time; a lapsed RNT means the listing is no longer properly registered.
  • Platforms are involved. Law 2068 of 2020 requires digital platforms such as Airbnb and Booking.com to stop publishing or remove listings from providers without an active RNT when the authorities ask them to. That is why Airbnb asks Colombian hosts for an RNT number.

The Medellín Chamber of Commerce publishes a step-by-step RNT guide if you want to see the process yourself.

A change to watch in 2026

During 2026, MinCIT published a draft decree on the RNT for public comment. As reported in the press, it would require an individual RNT for each tourist-home unit rather than one registration covering a portfolio, and tighten obligations for platforms. Industry groups have asked for changes or withdrawal. As of November 2026 we could not confirm a final, signed version, so treat this as "pending" and ask us for the latest status.

Requirement 2: your building must allow it

This is where many deals go wrong. If the apartment is in a building or conjunto under the horizontal property regime (propiedad horizontal), which is most apartments in El Poblado and Laureles, Decree 1836 of 2021 says the tourist-home use must be authorized in the building's bylaws (reglamento de propiedad horizontal). Building administrators are also expected to report units used as tourist homes without authorization or registration.

MinCIT has also pointed out that the use must be compatible with local land-use rules. In practice, before you buy for short-term rental income, ask for the building's reglamento and check whether tourist rental is expressly allowed. A great apartment in a building that does not permit it is a long-term rental, not an Airbnb.

Requirement 3: guest registration (TRA and SIRE)

Once you are operating, every stay creates two reporting duties:

  • TRA (Tarjeta de Registro de Alojamiento). Law 2068 of 2020 replaced the old hotel registration card with the accommodation registration card, and MinCIT's Resolution 700 of 2021 put it into use. Each guest is registered in MinCIT's online system, linked to your RNT.
  • SIRE (Migración Colombia). Lodging providers must report foreign guests to Migración Colombia through the SIRE system, which is used for immigration control.

These are separate systems run by separate agencies, and both need to be kept up to date. It is one of the most time-consuming parts of running a rental, and one of the easiest to forget when you live abroad.

Requirement 4: tax registration

Rental income in Colombia has tax consequences. As a starting point, the operator generally needs a RUT (tax ID) with DIAN, and income from the property must be declared. How income tax, VAT and withholding apply depends on whether you are a resident or non-resident, a person or a company, and how the property is operated. This is exactly where a foreign owner should get advice specific to their situation.

What happens if you skip the paperwork?

Operating without an RNT, or in a building that does not allow it, exposes the owner to sanctions under tourism law, which can include fines, temporary closure and cancellation of the registration, plus the platform removing the listing. The Alcaldía de Medellín has made informal tourist homes a public enforcement priority. Beyond penalties, an unregistered unit is harder to insure, harder to sell as an income property, and can create problems with neighbors and the building administration.

A simple checklist before you list

  1. Confirm the building's reglamento de propiedad horizontal allows tourist rental.
  2. Get your RUT with DIAN.
  3. Register the property in the RNT (free, online) and note the renewal window: 1 January to 31 March every year.
  4. Set up TRA reporting with MinCIT and SIRE reporting with Migración Colombia.
  5. Add the RNT number to your Airbnb, Booking.com, Expedia or VRBO listing.
  6. Keep an eye on the 2026 RNT decree and renew on time.

FAQ

Is Airbnb banned in Medellín? No. As of November 2026 there is no citywide ban. Short-term rental is legal when the property has an active RNT, the building's bylaws allow it, and guests are reported through TRA and SIRE. The city does inspect and sanction informal operators.

How much does the RNT cost? According to MinCIT, registering and renewing the RNT is free. It is done online at rnt.confecamaras.co and must be renewed between 1 January and 31 March each year.

Can a foreigner who lives abroad run an Airbnb in Medellín? Foreign owners can own property in Colombia and rent it out, but the compliance duties (RNT, TRA, SIRE, tax) still apply and need someone on the ground. Many owners work with a local property manager. Tax treatment depends on your residency, so get specific advice.
