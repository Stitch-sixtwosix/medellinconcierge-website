How to Rent a Furnished Apartment in Medellín Without a Fiador

7 November 2026 · 6 min read · Medellín Concierge

If you have looked for an apartment in Medellín for a few months, you have probably met the word fiador. Many Colombian landlords and rental agencies ask for one, and for a foreigner or a remote worker who just arrived, it can feel like a locked door. The good news: there are several legitimate ways to rent a furnished apartment in Medellín without a Colombian co-signer. This guide explains what a fiador is, what the usual alternatives are, which rules apply (as of November 2026), and what to check before you pay anything.

This is general information for renters, not legal advice. Rules and practices change, so confirm the details of any contract before you sign.

What is a fiador (or codeudor)?

A fiador or codeudor is a co-signer: a person who signs the lease with you and promises to pay if you don't. For traditional unfurnished leases, agencies often ask for a fiador who owns property in Colombia or has a verifiable local income. Colombia's residential lease law, Ley 820 de 2003, mentions co-debtors directly; for example, when a lease is written, the landlord must give a signed copy to the tenant and to any co-debtor.

For newcomers the problem is simple: you don't yet know anyone in Colombia who can sign for you. That is why the alternatives below matter.

Option 1: A rental insurance policy (póliza de arrendamiento)

Instead of a person, many landlords accept an insurance policy. A póliza de arrendamiento is an insurance product that protects the landlord if the tenant does not pay rent and related obligations such as building fees and utilities. The insurer reviews the applicant first, usually looking at income and credit history (El Espectador, La República).

What to know:

  • Approval depends on the insurer's review, which can be harder without a Colombian credit history.
  • Who pays for the policy is agreed in the contract, so ask before you sign.
  • The policy protects the landlord, not your belongings. If you want cover for your own things, that is a separate renter's insurance.

Option 2: Furnished monthly rentals from an owner or manager

Furnished apartments rented by the month, directly from an owner or a professional manager, are often the most practical route for travellers, remote workers and people who are still deciding where to live. These rentals are usually set up for people arriving from abroad: the apartment comes with furniture, kitchen equipment and internet, and the booking process is simpler than a traditional lease.

Requirements vary from one property to another, so always ask directly what is needed: identification, a signed contract, advance payment, or anything else. Don't assume that every furnished rental skips the fiador, and don't assume that every one requires it.

Option 3: Paying rent in advance

Some landlords accept advance rent instead of a co-signer, especially for shorter terms. If you go this way:

  • Put the agreement in a written contract that states how many months you prepaid and what happens if you leave early.
  • Pay by a traceable method and keep receipts.
  • Never prepay for an apartment you have not verified (see the checklist below).

What the law says about deposits (as of November 2026)

Here is something many renters don't know. For residential urban leases covered by Ley 820 de 2003, Article 16 prohibits requiring cash deposits or cauciones reales (collateral) to guarantee the contract, and the law sets sanctions for landlords who break this rule (Ley 820 de 2003). Article 15 allows a separate guarantee related to utility bills. In July 2026, Bogotá's housing authority published a legal opinion on how Articles 15 and 16 apply (Secretaría Distrital del Hábitat).

Whether a specific furnished or temporary rental falls under Ley 820 depends on how the contract is set up. If a deposit is requested, ask what it is for, how it is documented and when it is returned, and consult a qualified lawyer if you have doubts.

Short stays vs. monthly leases: the RNT

In Colombia, properties offered as tourist accommodation (vivienda turística) must be registered in the National Tourism Registry (RNT), and platforms remind hosts of this obligation (Airbnb Newsroom). Colombia's Superintendence of Industry and Commerce has said that the current definition of tourist housing does not rely on length of stay alone (SIC concept, April 2024).

For you as a renter, the practical takeaway is simple: for short stays, check that the listing shows an RNT number, and for monthly stays, make sure you receive a clear written contract.

Checklist before you pay anything

  1. Verify who you are dealing with. Ask for the owner's or company's name and confirm it. Anyone can request a property's certificado de tradición y libertad from the Superintendencia de Notariado y Registro to see who the registered owner is.
  2. See the apartment, in person or by live video call, not just photos.
  3. Get a written contract with the monthly price, what it includes (utilities, internet, building fees, cleaning), the dates, and the cancellation terms.
  4. Ask about the building's rules. Buildings in Colombia have a reglamento de propiedad horizontal that may affect guests, pets and moving in.
  5. Pay by a traceable method and keep every receipt.
  6. Document the inventory. Take photos of the furniture and condition on arrival.

FAQ

Can a foreigner rent an apartment in Medellín without a fiador?

Yes, it is possible. Common alternatives are a rental insurance policy (póliza de arrendamiento), a furnished monthly rental from an owner or manager, or an agreement to pay rent in advance. Requirements vary by property, so ask before you commit.

Is it legal for a landlord in Colombia to ask for a deposit?

For residential urban leases covered by Ley 820 de 2003, Article 16 prohibits requiring cash deposits or collateral, as of November 2026. Whether a specific furnished or temporary rental is covered depends on the contract. For a specific case, consult a qualified lawyer.

What should I check before paying for a furnished apartment in Medellín?

Confirm who owns or manages the property, see the apartment in person or by live video, get a written contract that lists what is included, check the building's rules, and pay only by a traceable method.
