Working Remotely From Medellín: Internet, Coworking, Time Zone and Where to Live (2026)

18 November 2026 · 7 min read · Medellín Concierge

Short answer: Medellín works well for remote workers. Colombia stays on UTC-5 all year, so you overlap the whole US working day; fixed internet in the metro area is fast by Colombian standards; and the city has a public innovation hub, private coworking spaces and hundreds of free public Wi-Fi points. The things that make or break your month are specific to the apartment you pick: the real Wi-Fi speed, a proper desk, and a plan B if the power or connection drops.

This guide covers what to check before you book. For neighborhoods, read El Poblado vs Laureles vs Envigado: where to stay in Medellín for a month, and for how long you can legally stay, our Colombia digital nomad visa guide.

General information as of November 2026. Rules, prices and services change, so confirm details before you travel.

What time zone is Medellín in?

Colombia uses UTC-5 all year and does not use daylight saving time. The legal time was set by Decreto 2707 de 1982, and the Instituto Nacional de Metrología keeps and distributes Colombia's official time.

Because your home country may change its clocks while Colombia doesn't, the gap moves during the year:

For North American teams that means normal office hours. For European teams, expect early mornings: a 9:00 meeting in Madrid is 3:00 a.m. in Medellín in winter, so check your meeting schedule before you commit to a long stay.

Is the internet in Medellín good enough for video calls?

Generally yes, in a well-equipped apartment. In Ookla's Speedtest data for the second half of 2025, Envigado, the municipality just south of El Poblado, ranked among the fastest of Colombia's most populous cities for median fixed download speed, as reported by Finance Colombia.

A city median is not a promise for one apartment, though. Speed depends on the plan the owner pays for, whether the building has fiber, where the router sits and how many people share it. So:

  • Ask for a recent screenshot of a speed test taken inside the unit, showing both download and upload. Upload matters for video calls and file sharing.
  • Ask whether you can plug in a cable at the desk, which is usually more stable than Wi-Fi for long calls.
  • Keep a local SIM with a data plan as a hotspot backup.

Where can I work outside the apartment?

You have three kinds of options:

  1. Ruta N, Medellín's public innovation agency, runs a complex in the north of the city with coworking areas, offices and meeting rooms. See its workspaces page for current options and who they are for.
  2. Private coworking spaces, found in El Poblado, Laureles and other areas, usually sell day passes and monthly memberships. Prices and hours change, so check directly with each space.
  3. Free public Wi-Fi: as of mid-2026 the Alcaldía reported hundreds of public Wi-Fi points across the city, in places such as parks and public buildings. It's handy for email between errands; for client calls or anything confidential, use your apartment's connection or your phone's hotspot plus a VPN.

Will my laptop and chargers work?

Colombia uses Type A and Type B plugs (the same flat-pin plugs as the US and Canada) at about 110 to 120 V and 60 Hz, as Remitly's guide to electrical outlets in Colombia explains. Most laptop and phone chargers are rated 100 to 240 V, so visitors from Europe or the UK usually need only a plug adapter, not a voltage converter. Check the label on your charger.

What about power cuts in late 2026 and early 2027?

This one is worth knowing this season. Colombia relies heavily on hydropower, and with a strong El Niño forecast, the grid operator XM has warned of a risk of energy rationing, and EPM, Medellín's utility, raised its rationing-risk alert to orange, naming early 2027 among the most sensitive months. In the most recent reports we found (September 2026), no national rationing had been scheduled.

None of that means outages will happen, but if your income depends on being online, it is sensible to:

  • ask whether the building has a backup generator and what it powers (lifts and common areas only, or apartments too);
  • carry a power bank that can charge your laptop, or a small UPS for the router;
  • check EPM and local news before key deadlines.

FAQ

Is Medellín good for digital nomads?
For many remote workers, yes: it shares the US Eastern time zone for part of the year and is only an hour off it for the rest, the metro area has fast fixed internet by Colombian standards, and there are coworking spaces and public Wi-Fi. The apartment you choose matters most, so check its speed, desk and backup options before you book.

What internet speed do I need for video calls?
That depends on your video app and how many people share the connection, so check your provider's own requirements. What matters most is a stable connection with good upload speed, so ask for an in-unit speed test and use a cable when you can.

Do I need a visa to work remotely from Medellín?
It depends on your nationality and how long you stay. Read our Colombia digital nomad visa guide and confirm with Cancillería and Migración Colombia before you travel. This is general information, not legal advice.
