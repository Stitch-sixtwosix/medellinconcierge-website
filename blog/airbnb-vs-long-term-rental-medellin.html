Airbnb vs Long-Term Rental in Medellín: Which Is Right for Your Property?

12 November 2026 · 6 min read · Medellín Concierge

Short answer: neither option wins for every owner. Short-term rental (Airbnb and similar platforms) can earn more per night, but it needs an RNT registration, explicit permission in the building's rules (reglamento de propiedad horizontal), a location where land-use rules allow it, and hands-on daily operations. A traditional long-term lease gives steadier income with far less work, under the clear rules of Law 820 of 2003. In Medellín today, the deciding question is usually whether your building and your zone allow tourist rentals at all.

This guide is current as of November 2026 and gives you a comparison table and a checklist. It is general information: rules change, so confirm the details for your property, and for tax or legal questions consult a qualified professional (we are not tax or legal advisors).

What is the difference between short-term and long-term rentals in Medellín?

  • Short-term rental ("vivienda turística"): stays by the night or week, usually through Airbnb, Booking.com, Expedia or VRBO. Colombia treats this as a tourism service, so it requires the National Tourism Registry (RNT), reporting foreign guests to Migración Colombia (SIRE) and the hotel registration card (TRA). We explain each step in Is Airbnb legal in Medellín? RNT, TRA and SIRE.
  • Long-term rental ("arriendo de vivienda urbana"): one tenant, usually on a lease of a year or more, governed by Law 820 of 2003. Among other things, the rent can only rise every 12 months and by no more than 100% of the previous year's CPI (Article 20). See our guide to long-term rental management in Medellín.

Does my building and neighborhood allow Airbnb?

Ask this before you look at returns. In its Q&A ¿Qué está permitido y qué no?, the Medellín mayor's office (Alcaldía) explains that:

  1. In apartment buildings and gated complexes, tourist rental must be expressly authorized in the building's horizontal-property rules.
  2. The RNT does not replace land-use rules. According to the Alcaldía, Article 87 of the Police Code requires both.

Enforcement is also stepping up. According to the Alcaldía de Medellín, there were 408 inspections of tourist homes between 2024 and 2026 to date, and 105 were temporarily suspended for breaking the rules. Tourist-home operators registered in the RNT grew from 521 in 2019 to 8,322 in 2026.

The city's land-use plan (POT) revision is redrawing the map too. Press reports from early November 2026 say the City Council approved the revision in its second debate (see RCN Radio and, on the earlier process, Publimetro). The Alcaldía says in Vivienda turística y POT: dónde sí, dónde no that the plan does not ban the activity but concentrates it in mixed-use zones and areas near metro stations, with restrictions in mainly residential, low-mix zones. We had not seen the final published text when we wrote this, so check your exact address before you buy or convert.

Which earns more in Medellín?

It depends. Press analyses such as Portafolio's and Bancolombia's blog agree that short-term rental can out-earn a traditional lease, in exchange for more management and more requirements. It also carries more costs: cleaning, utilities, internet, platform fees, replacing linens and supplies, and management. We don't publish yield figures, because they vary widely by neighborhood, building and unit type.

To compare with real numbers, run this exercise for your own unit:

  • Short-term: expected nightly rate × booked nights per year, minus platform fees, cleaning, utilities, supplies, management and taxes.
  • Long-term: monthly rent × 12, minus expected empty months, management, maintenance and taxes.
  • Compare net, not gross, and count the time you (or your manager) will spend on each.

Checklist: which option fits you?

Tick the ones that apply:

  • The building rules expressly allow tourist rentals.
  • The address is in a zone where land use allows tourist accommodation.
  • You can get the RNT and keep up with SIRE and TRA (or hand them to a manager).
  • The unit is furnished, or you are willing to invest in furnishing, photos and a listing.
  • You prefer higher income even if it varies month to month.
  • You want to use the property yourself a few weeks a year.

If you ticked nearly all of them, short-term rental may make sense. If either of the first two fails, the realistic option today is a long-term lease (or a furnished monthly rental under whatever rules apply; check with a qualified professional).

Can I switch from Airbnb to a long-term lease, or the other way round?

Yes, but plan it. Moving to a long-term lease means following Law 820's rules on lease length, renewal and termination. Moving to short-term means confirming building rules and land use first, then getting the RNT. Either way, review the tax side with an accountant.

FAQ

Is it legal to run an Airbnb in Medellín?

Yes, if you meet the requirements: a valid RNT, SIRE reporting, TRA, express authorization in the building rules and land use that allows it, according to the Alcaldía de Medellín (as of November 2026). The POT revision may change where it is allowed, so check your exact address.

How much can long-term rent go up each year?

As of November 2026, Law 820 of 2003 (Article 20) lets the landlord raise the rent every 12 months by up to 100% of the previous year's CPI, which DANE publishes.

What happens if I run an Airbnb without an RNT?

You risk sanctions. Law 2068 of 2020 requires platforms not to publish, or to remove, listings without a registration, and the Alcaldía has suspended tourist homes for violations. For your specific case, consult a lawyer.
