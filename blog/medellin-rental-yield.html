Medellín Rental Yield: How to Calculate Your Real Return Before You Buy (2026)

15 November 2026 · 6 min read · Medellín Concierge

Short answer: rental yield in Medellín is your annual rent divided by the total cost of the property (gross yield), and, more usefully, your annual rent minus the year's real costs divided by the same amount (net yield). Global Property Guide puts the average gross rental yield for housing in Colombia at 7.01% in Q1 2026. Net yields are lower once costs are subtracted. But an average is not your apartment: the number that matters is the one built from your own figures.

This guide, current as of November 2026, walks you through the formula, the costs foreign and local owners most often forget, and the questions to ask before you buy or choose between short-term and long-term renting. It is general information, not financial, legal or tax advice. We are not tax advisors, so please consult a qualified professional about your own situation.

What is the difference between gross and net rental yield?

  • Gross yield = (monthly rent × 12) ÷ total property cost × 100. Good for quick comparisons between apartments or neighborhoods.
  • Net yield = (annual income − annual costs) ÷ total investment × 100. This is what you actually keep.

Your total investment is more than the purchase price. Add closing and registration costs, initial repairs and, if you furnish the unit, furniture. Published averages such as Global Property Guide's Colombia figures are gross: they ignore taxes and operating costs.

Which costs should you subtract to get net yield?

These are the costs that weigh most on a Medellín apartment, and where to find the real figure for your unit:

For short-term rentals, also add utilities, internet, cleaning between guests, linen replacement and platform fees. In a long-term lease, whether the owner or the tenant pays the HOA fee depends on the contract, so check it before you run the numbers.

Worked example: how do you calculate it step by step?

The example below is hypothetical, with round numbers to show the method. It is not market data and not a promise of results.

  • Total property cost: COP 400,000,000
  • Monthly rent: COP 2,400,000, so COP 28,800,000 a year
  • Gross yield: 28,800,000 ÷ 400,000,000 = 7.2%

Now subtract the year's costs (also hypothetical): one month of vacancy (COP 2,400,000), an HOA fee of COP 400,000 a month (COP 4,800,000), predial (COP 1,600,000) and maintenance (COP 800,000). Total: COP 9,600,000.

  • Net income: 28,800,000 − 9,600,000 = COP 19,200,000
  • Net yield: 19,200,000 ÷ 400,000,000 = 4.8%, before management fees and income tax.

That is 2.4 points lost to costs that never appear in a listing. It is why you should ask for the real bills before you buy.

Does Airbnb or a long-term rental give a better yield in Medellín?

It depends on the property and, above all, on whether short-term rental is allowed there. Airbnb income is the nightly rate times the nights booked, and both move with the season, the neighborhood and the quality of the listing. Costs are higher too, and the work is daily.

Before you count on a nightly rate, check three things: that the building rules (reglamento de propiedad horizontal) expressly allow tourist rentals, that land-use rules at your address allow them, and that you can hold an active RNT registration. We cover these in Airbnb requirements in Medellín. To compare the two models in depth, read Airbnb vs long-term rental in Medellín.

How much can rent go up each year in Medellín?

As of November 2026, for urban housing, Colombia's Law 820 of 2003 caps the annual rent increase at the previous year's consumer price inflation (IPC). According to Portafolio, citing DANE, 2025 inflation closed at 5.1%, which is the cap for 2026 increases. This matters for your math: in a long-term lease, income grows at most with inflation, so your yield depends heavily on the price you pay. Quick checklist before you buy to rent

  • Ask for the latest HOA statement and predial bill.
  • Read the building rules: do they allow short-term rentals?
  • Calculate net yield with at least one month of vacancy a year.
  • Add closing costs, registration, repairs and furniture to your total investment.
  • Ask an accountant how rental income is declared in your case.
  • Compare your result with public averages, but never treat them as a guarantee.

FAQ

What is a good rental yield in Medellín?
There is no single number. As a reference, Global Property Guide reports an average gross rental yield of 7.01% for Colombia in Q1 2026. What matters is how your net yield compares with your other investment options and your tolerance for risk.

Are the yields quoted online gross or net?
Almost always gross: annual rent divided by price. They leave out HOA fees, predial, vacancy, maintenance and taxes, so your net yield will be lower.

Do foreign owners pay tax on rental income in Colombia?
Rental income from a Colombian property is generally taxable, and how it is taxed depends on your tax residency and situation. Check with DIAN and a qualified Colombian tax advisor; we do not give tax advice.
