Can foreigners own and rent out property in Colombia?

Yes. Colombia’s Constitution (Article 100) gives foreigners the same civil rights as Colombians, and foreign owners can rent out an apartment in Medellín like any local owner. When money from abroad is used to buy, it is normally channelled through the foreign-exchange market and registered with the Banco de la República; ProColombia explains how to register a foreign investment. Keeping that registration in order matters later, for example when you want to send sale proceeds back abroad.

Renting to tourists adds three conditions: your building’s reglamento de propiedad horizontal must allow short stays, the property needs an active Registro Nacional de Turismo (RNT) renewed every year, and every guest has to be registered (TRA) and foreign guests reported to Migración Colombia (SIRE). Taxes, registrations and compliance for foreign owners

RNT, SIRE and TRA are included in our management: we register the property, renew it and file the guest reports at every check-in, which is exactly when an owner abroad can’t act. Rental income earned in Colombia can also have Colombian tax obligations for non-resident owners: the DIAN taxes non-residents on Colombian-source income, and its guidance covers withholding on rent paid to non-residents (DIAN, Oficio 10721 de 2025). We manage registrations, not taxes: we don't give tax or legal advice, so confirm yours with a qualified Colombian accountant. How to vet a property manager from abroad: a checklist

Before you hand over the keys, check these points with any manager, including us:

  • The company is active. Search its name or NIT in the national business registry, RUES.
  • The tourism registration is current. For short stays, look up the RNT on the public RNT system and check it is active, not just that a number exists.
  • The landlord registration exists. For long-term leases in Medellín, ask for the matrícula de arrendador number.
  • The fees are in writing. Ask what the commission covers, what is charged on top and whether IVA is added.
  • You know when you get paid. Ask for the payout date, how payments reach you and in which currency.
  • Reports show every line. Monthly statements should list each income and expense so you can check them against deposits.
  • Repairs have an approval limit. Agree the amount the manager can spend without asking you.
  • Guests are covered 24/7. Ask who answers a guest or tenant at 2 a.m., and in which languages.

Frequently asked questions

Can a foreigner own and rent out property in Colombia?

Yes. Colombia’s Constitution (Article 100) gives foreigners the same civil rights as Colombians, and foreign owners can rent out their apartment. Short stays also need an active RNT and a building that allows them (as of November 2026). Confirm your own case with a qualified Colombian lawyer.

Do I need to be in Colombia to hire you?

No. We start with a free consultation by WhatsApp or email, and once your property is live you follow bookings, statements and payouts in your owner portal. We handle guests, check-ins, cleaning, maintenance and registrations on the ground.

How much does property management cost for a foreign owner?

The same as for any owner: 12% of rental income + IVA for long stays and 17% + IVA for short stays on Basic, or 15% and 20% + IVA on Premium. No upfront fees. See the full fee breakdown.

Who pays the building administration and utilities?

On the Premium plan we pay your property tax, HOA fees, utilities and internet for you and send a monthly accounting report. On Basic, you keep paying those bills yourself.

How do I see what my property earns?

In your owner portal you can see bookings, statements and payouts at any time. Each month we pay the previous month’s earnings within the first 5 business days, with GAAP statements. See how we report your earnings.

Do foreign owners pay tax in Colombia on rental income?

The DIAN taxes non-residents on Colombian-source income, which includes rent from a property in Colombia, and withholding may apply (as of November 2026). We manage RNT, SIRE and TRA, not taxes, so confirm your obligations with a qualified Colombian accountant.
